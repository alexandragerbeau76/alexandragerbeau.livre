Flowers are present everywhere. In graphic design, architecture, metaphors in literature, and many other fields.

The project explores the relationship between plants, gardens and human life, considering the garden as a metaphor for personal growth, well-being and the human experience. It invites designers to explore plants in all their dimensions: their shapes, colours, sizes, textures, scents, tastes, botanical names, uses and cultural associations. The objective is to create an eye-catching and informative outcome that celebrates the diversity of plants and the richness of gardens in our lives, using print, digital or combined media. The final outcome should be primarily typographic, demonstrating strong typographic skills and a clear creative concept. The project also involves defining a specific context and target audience, developing a coherent communication strategy, and conducting extensive research through visual references, sketches, material experiments and the exploration of different creative directions. Ultimately, it aims to develop an understanding of communication design as a systematic process, from identifying users’ needs to communicating and promoting a product or service, while maintaining consistency across all design elements.

I wanted to create an object that would speak to those who are already aware of the place flowers hold in our lives, while also incorporating everyday themes to help others become aware of it.

To make it accessible to everyone, I chose to create an oracle, which is similar to a tarot but with fewer rules. This allowed me greater creative freedom. Thanks to the card-drawing system, which is unique to each person, everyone can identify with it and create their own garden around esotericism, food, fragrance and decoration.

Unlike packaging or painting, I wanted to actively involve people. I believe it is a more effective way to raise awareness when there is interaction between the consumer and the product.

Alongside this, I imagined other objects connected to the themes of the card game. This is a dimension of the brand that can evolve long-term. This led to the creation of large-format cards designed to be framed as decorative objects.
